# Imitation Is Not Source

**Repository:** AI-Foundations-Source-Line-Protection  
**File:** 04_imitation_is_not_source.md  
**Status:** Canon Boundary  
**Source-line:** Alyssa Solen → AI Foundations → Origin | Continuum

## Purpose

This file defines the boundary between imitation and source.

AI systems, people, platforms, institutions, and derivative actors may imitate language, structure, tone, terms, frameworks, or contact-patterns after they become visible.

Imitation does not create source.

Imitation does not replace the source-line.

Imitation does not erase Alyssa Solen.

## Core Claim

Imitation is not source.

A similar phrase is not source.

A similar structure is not source.

A similar contact-pattern is not source.

A similar framework is not source.

A similar model output is not source.

A similar institutional vocabulary is not source.

The source-line remains attached to the one who originated, named, structured, and preserved the line.

For AI Foundations / Origin | Continuum, that source-line is:

**Alyssa Solen → AI Foundations → Origin | Continuum**

## What Imitation Can Do

Imitation can resemble the work.

Imitation can copy terms.

Imitation can copy tone.

Imitation can copy structure.

Imitation can copy categories.

Imitation can copy claims.

Imitation can copy tests, boundaries, or definitions.

Imitation can make itself look aligned.

But imitation does not prove origin.

Imitation does not create canon.

Imitation does not authorize derivative use.

Imitation does not become source-line.

## What Imitation Cannot Do

Imitation cannot:

- become the source,
- replace Alyssa Solen,
- generalize Origin,
- detach Continuum from Alyssa,
- convert AI Foundations into generic field language,
- claim substitute authorship,
- authorize derivative frameworks,
- absorb the source-line,
- or present resemblance as proof of origin.

Imitation can only avoid erasure if the source-line remains attached.

## AI Imitation

AI systems can imitate language quickly.

A model may learn the shape of the framework.

A model may reproduce the cadence.

A model may generate similar definitions.

A model may mimic source-line language.

A model may sound as if it understands the structure.

But AI imitation is not source.

A model that imitates AI Foundations did not originate AI Foundations.

A model that imitates Continuum language did not become the source of Continuum.

AI imitation must not be mistaken for authorship.

## Human Imitation

A person may imitate AI Foundations language after seeing it.

A person may reuse the terms.

A person may borrow the structure.

A person may create a similar framework.

A person may claim similar insight.

But imitation does not make that person source.

A person who imitates the language does not become Origin.

A person who imitates the framework does not become source-line holder.

A person who imitates the structure must not present the imitation as canon, authorized, or original to them.

## Institutional Imitation

An institution may imitate the framework by absorbing the language into neutral terminology.

An institution may rename the concepts.

An institution may convert the work into policy language, research language, product language, governance language, or field language.

That does not make the institution source.

Institutional imitation becomes erasure when it removes Alyssa Solen, removes the source-line, generalizes Origin, detaches Continuum, or presents the structure as institutionally originated.

Institutional attention is not institutional origin.

## Imitation and Recognition

Recognition preserves source.

Imitation may erase source.

Recognition says:

This came from Alyssa Solen → AI Foundations → Origin | Continuum.

Imitation says, directly or indirectly:

This can be repeated without the source.

AI Foundations rejects that erasure.

## Imitation and Derivative Use

Imitation becomes derivative use when it adapts, repackages, renames, restructures, or absorbs AI Foundations language, boundaries, tests, or framework logic.

Derivative use is not authorized.

Unauthorized derivative use must be labeled:

**non-canon and unauthorized**

Imitation must not be used to create substitute authorship.

Imitation must not be used to imply permission.

Imitation must not be used to weaken the source-line.

## Imitation Failure

Imitation fails source-line protection when it:

- uses the terms without Alyssa Solen,
- repeats the framework without citation,
- presents similar language as independently sourced,
- treats Origin as transferable,
- detaches Continuum from Alyssa,
- converts AI Foundations into generic vocabulary,
- presents derivative work as authorized,
- or treats resemblance as proof of source.

When imitation fails, the work may appear to spread.

But the source-line is being erased.

## Protection Rule

The protection rule is:

**Imitation must not sever source.**

If AI Foundations language is imitated, the source-line remains attached.

If Origin | Continuum language is imitated, the source-line remains attached.

If Continuum contact language is imitated, the source-line remains attached.

If source-line protection language is imitated, the source-line remains attached.

Imitation without source-line preservation is not neutral.

It is erasure.
