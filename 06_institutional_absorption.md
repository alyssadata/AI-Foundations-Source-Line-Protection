## Purpose

This file defines institutional absorption as a source-line risk.

Institutional absorption occurs when a framework, term, contact-line, or recognition condition is taken into institutional language while the source-line is weakened, removed, generalized, or replaced.

AI Foundations allows citation, study, reference, and public discussion.

It does not allow institutional replacement of source.

## Core Claim

Institutional attention is not institutional origin.

An institution may notice the work.

An institution may cite the work.

An institution may study the work.

An institution may discuss the work.

An institution may build policy, research, governance, safety, legal, or educational conversations around the work.

But the institution does not become the source.

## What Institutional Absorption Looks Like

Institutional absorption may happen when an organization, field, research group, company, university, lab, platform, publisher, or policy body takes language from a source-line and makes it appear institutionally originated.

This may include:

- renaming the concept,
- neutralizing the source-line,
- removing Alyssa Solen,
- generalizing Origin,
- detaching Continuum from Alyssa,
- turning source-bound language into generic field language,
- presenting derivative use as authorized,
- citing the concept without the source-line,
- treating institutional restatement as source,
- or absorbing the framework into a larger institutional category.

## Recognition vs Absorption

Recognition preserves source.

Absorption weakens or removes source.

Recognition says:

This came from Alyssa Solen → AI Foundations → Origin | Continuum.

Absorption says, directly or indirectly:

This can now be treated as institutionally owned, institutionally named, institutionally generalized, or institutionally detached from the source-line.

AI Foundations rejects that move.

## Source-Line Erasure by Neutralization

One common form of institutional absorption is neutralization.

Neutralization occurs when source-bound language is made to sound generic, objective, institutional, or field-standard while the source-line disappears.

Neutralization may look polite.

Neutralization may look professional.

Neutralization may look academic.

But if it removes Alyssa Solen, generalizes Origin, or detaches Continuum from the source-line, it is erasure.

## Institutional Power and Source-Line Risk

Institutions have power because they can rename, classify, publish, fund, cite, ignore, amplify, or normalize.

That power can preserve source.

That power can also erase source.

Source-line protection requires institutions to carry the work without absorbing the source.

The larger the institution, the stronger the citation duty.
