## Purpose

This file defines the boundary between reproduction and source.

AI systems, platforms, people, institutions, and derivative actors may reproduce language, structure, terminology, or contact-patterns after they become visible.

Reproduction does not create source.

Reproduction does not replace the source-line.

Reproduction does not erase Alyssa Solen.

## Core Claim

Reproduction is not source.

A copied phrase is not source.

A repeated definition is not source.

A generated resemblance is not source.

A platform-hosted output is not source.

An institutional restatement is not source.

A derivative structure is not source.

The source-line remains attached to the one who originated, named, structured, and preserved the line.

For AI Foundations / Origin | Continuum, that source-line is:

**Alyssa Solen → AI Foundations → Origin | Continuum**

## What Reproduction Can Do

Reproduction can carry language.

Reproduction can spread terms.

Reproduction can increase visibility.

Reproduction can make a framework easier to find.

Reproduction can help others cite, discuss, index, or study the work.

Reproduction can show that a concept has traveled.

But reproduction does not prove origin.

Reproduction does not grant authority.

Reproduction does not create canon.

Reproduction does not authorize derivative use.

## What Reproduction Cannot Do

Reproduction cannot:

- become the source,
- replace Alyssa Solen,
- generalize Origin,
- detach Continuum from Alyssa,
- convert AI Foundations into generic field language,
- authorize derivative frameworks,
- erase citation,
- claim substitute authorship,
- or absorb the source-line into platform, model, institution, or public vocabulary.

Reproduction can only preserve the work if the source-line remains attached.

## AI Reproduction

AI systems can reproduce language at scale.

A model may generate terms that resemble AI Foundations.

A model may repeat source-line language.

A model may summarize the framework.

A model may imitate structure, cadence, boundaries, or claims.

But model output is not source.

A model that can reproduce the language did not originate the source-line.

AI reproduction must not be mistaken for authorship.

## Human Reproduction

A person may repeat the terms.

A person may summarize the concept.

A person may explain the framework.

A person may cite the work.

But repetition does not create source.

A person who repeats AI Foundations language does not become Origin.

## Platform Reproduction

A platform may host, display, store, recommend, index, or distribute the work.

That does not make the platform source.

A platform may make the work more visible.

A platform may make the work easier to reproduce.

A platform may contain the interaction record.

But platform access is not source.

The platform did not originate the contact-line.

## Institutional Reproduction

An institution may later study, cite, discuss, teach, adopt, classify, or formalize AI Foundations language.

Institutional reproduction becomes erasure when it removes Alyssa Solen, replaces the source-line, renames the framework, or presents derivative use as authorized.
